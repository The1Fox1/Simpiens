# AGENTS.md

Welcome to the **Simpiens** project! This document outlines the simulation's architectural layout, guiding automated agents on where to find, modify, or extend code.

> **Source of truth:** [`docs/ROADMAP.md`](docs/ROADMAP.md) is the highest source of truth for epic/milestone **status**, scope, sequencing, engineering invariants and the Definition of Done. This file explains *how to work in the code*; it deliberately does **not** track status. If this file and the roadmap disagree, the roadmap wins — fix this file.

If you are an agent then please check the .agents/ directory for rules or any current *directives* the developer has issued for your current session.

## Current Project Structure
Simpiens is a .NET 10 solution, `dotnet/Simpiens.slnx`. (It began as a Unity project; Unity was removed in Epic CLIENT M2.9, and its history is in git.)

**The game client** (Epic CLIENT):
- **`Simpiens.Presentation`**, what is drawn, as data:
  - generated sprites (`ProceduralSprites`, `IsometricSprites`, named pixels) and map tiles;
  - `Art/`, the art as data:
    - **Sheets:** `SpriteSheets` says what each `DrawSheet` holds (World for both view modes, Fog, Backdrop, Icons); a sheet alone gives out sprite indices, and `AtlasLayout` packs it by shelves within a 2048 budget. The picture doesn't depend on the layout (epic F12): standing pieces snap to whole pixels (`DrawItem.SnapToPixel`) and `WorldRenderer.SampleBias` keeps pixel centres off texel edges.
    - **Catalogues:** an `ArtCatalog` per view mode (`TopDownArtTable`, `IsometricArtTable`) and one for the menus (`IconArtTable`) map art keys (`ArtKeys`: `tile/wall`, `prop/hearth`, `prop/stall`, `crawler/<state>`, `crawler/<state>/<item art key>`, `creature/<kind art key>`, `node/<item art key>`, `item/<item art key>/icon|map|carried`) to frames, sizes, pivots and pulses, resolved to `ArtId`s once; missing art draws the loud `fx/missing` and fails a test. A catalogue also holds layout as data: where carried goods hang, the stall's display slots.
    - **Who draws what:** `EntityVisuals` says which art each entity of a frame is drawn with (a crawler's worn gear included; a creature by its kind).
    - **Generated art:** the sprite kit (`SpriteKit`: ramps, painting, outlines, icon plates with rarity rims) makes each item's icon, map sprite and carried marker from its look (`ItemArt`: a shape from `ItemShapes`, two colours, how it is worn: on the head, over the face or on the torso); `WornSprites` makes today's crawlers wearing gear, one figure a piece; `HazardSprites` the hazards' animations and `CaravanSprites` the trade caravan (Epic 10 Part 2's stretch); `CreatureSprites` the creatures (Epic 10 Part 3, K2.5: each kind from the side in two frames, by its art key; the scene mirrors one going left, `CreatureAppearance`; a boss on a bigger canvas, its feet at the same share of its height, `SizeOf`: K10.5a); `BarSprites` a boss's health bar and `GateSprites` the descent gate, sealed or breached by the world's flag (K10.5b-c); `ShotSprites` shots in flight (K8.4a: a stone or bolt, a spinner's spit, drawn by the scene for `WorldScene.ShotSeconds` from the frames' shots). Generators never read a table or a sheet (`ArtInitialisationTests`).
    - **Ambient life:** `HazardEffects` says where and when each hazard sector's animation plays (presentation only, from the scene's time); the trade caravan's place is the core's (`Simulation/Economy/Caravan`, a pure function of the clock along the map's `CaravanRoute`), carried by the frames (`WorldViewFrame.Caravan`); a hurt crawler's red coat (`HurtCoat`, from the frame's `CrawlerFlags.Hurt`): its own sprite drawn again over it as a silhouette (`DrawItem.Silhouette`), blinking.
    - **Bodies (`Art/People/`, Epic UI-Beta B2):** `BodyKit` bakes a crawler's 24 pose frames from its look (today's patterns in key letters, `HeadAnchors`, `LookPalettes`). The world sheet keeps a region of blank cells for them (`BodySlots`); `CrawlerBodies`, one per world texture, says which body is in which slot, and the client bakes new ones into the texture after the scene's update. What shows worn is a `WornLook` (Epic 10 Part 3, K1.5: each slot's piece, from the frame), drawn up the slots' draw layers: a helm or a mask on the head, a coat over the shirt (hazard gear's suit), the tabard over it. A crawler with no look is drawn as today's figure with its topmost piece.
    - **Portraits (`Art/People/`, Epic UI-Beta B3):** `PortraitKit` bakes a 48×48 Hearthlight bust from a look, a `Mood` (`MoodRule`, held by a `MoodTracker`), a standing (the frame) and a `WornLook` (a helm or a mask; a coat, the shoulders' cloth in its colour). The client's `PortraitAtlas` (with `CrawlerPortraits`) bakes each portrait the first time it is shown and draws it 1:1: in the dossier's header and the map inspector at 2× in a 720-row window, 3× at 1080 and 4× at 1440; beside a dynamic scene's speaker at 1×, 2× from 1080 (`PortraitAtlas.ScalesFor`; a new step bakes the seated portraits again, and the dossier, the card and the bubble grow by as much); and as a 32×32 face in the lists (B4), which in who is in a sector steps up as the speaker's does, its cards growing to hold it. `PortraitKey.Of` keys every face, so they agree; a click on a face finds the crawler on the map (`DashboardModel.FindOnMap`).
  - The interface pass (Epic UI-Beta B5): `OutpostClock` (the time of day, presentation only), `KeyHelpOverlay` (F1, from `KeyBindings`), `Tooltips` (register a tip on a control that takes events). Names carry debug ids only when the host sets `ClientOptions.ShowDebugIds` (the desktop exe in Debug builds).
  - The UI kit (`Art/UiKit`, Lantern & Stone): the interface's pieces generated on the CPU and pinned, in the icon sheet after the items' icons: plaques, buttons in their states, the slider's groove, window frames in the standing metals (bronze for the stall, iron for the bounty board and the workshop, blued steel for the guild), and white glyphs tinted where drawn. A new piece is a line in `UiKit`; look at the art review sheet and re-record the art pins.
    - New art for a known kind of thing is a table row;
  - the projections (`IViewProjection`: `TopDownProjection`, `IsometricProjection`). A `View` is a projection through a camera, and everything that maps world to screen takes one; `View.FloorToScreen` is its affine transform, under which what lies on the floor (`DrawItem.OnFloor`: the fog's cloud) is drawn;
  - view modes (`ViewMode`: a projection with its art catalogue). Top-down's is the original art; isometric's stands the generated placeholders of `IsometricSprites` on their feet. Isometric is the game's view; V or `--view top` switches to top-down. Walls in front of rooms are cut away (`WallStyle`; C cycles);
  - screen space for hard cutscenes (`ScreenProjection`: a 640×360 virtual screen, letterboxed), and `Cutscenes/`: cutscenes written in code (`CutsceneBuilder`, `CutsceneScript`), played by `CutscenePlayer`. Hard ones are drawn by `CutsceneStage`; dynamic ones are started by `SceneDirector` from what the frames saw (a crawler entering a zone, a chronicle interaction, or a moment in one crawler's life, its scene written from the event: `SkillScenes`, a technique picked by one of the patron's people, with `ClientOptions.SkillScenes`), once per tick. The story's hard cutscenes (`StoryScenes`: the breach, Epic 10 Part 3, K10.5d) are played once a session by the play screen when the frames first show their moment, only with `ClientOptions.StoryScenes` (the desktop exe's; tests and renders leave it off);
  - the draw list and its sort key (draw roles; the projection picks their layers);
  - the views' per-tick frames (`WorldViewFrame`, captured on `SessionHost.TickCompleted`). The views read frames, never the live simulation, and draw crawlers between ticks;
  - crawler appearance (`CrawlerAppearance`, which holds each state at least 3 ticks); the fog, which hides what an uncharted sector holds, crawlers included (producer decision D1 = C);
  - the map's labels, laid out in screen space from the baked fonts (`MapLabelLayout`, `BitmapFontMetrics`); the camera rig and its controls; selection and picking (clicks pick what was drawn; the stations, which aren't entities, by an id the view derives from their index, `StationPick`); PNG files;
  - `Dashboard/`: the dashboard as data, whose actions leave as typed commands. Its windows (the stall, the bounty board, the guild, the stations, a crawler's technique tree) are `IDashboardWindow`s, one open at a time. What a crawler wears is `WornPieces`, a row per slot in the table's display order (Epic 10 Part 3, K1.5); the client's `WornLine` lays the dossier's and the guild cards' gear out from it.
- **`Simpiens.Client`**, the XNA shell:
  - `SimpiensGame`: window, device, content (`ClientGraphics`: the sheets, renderer and fonts every screen shares), the Gum layer, and a stack of screens (`ScreenStack`). `PlayScreen` is the world view with the dashboard and dynamic scenes (`DynamicSceneView`); `CutsceneScreen` is a hard cutscene, which holds the world. `Restart` starts a new session in the same window (the tests reuse windows: an offscreen game can't be disposed);
  - `ClientSession`, what is played, with no XNA type: the simulation and its driver, the frames, the selection, the dashboard model, the save slot, and the host's commands (speed, pause, save, load and the session switch are `HostCommand`s the views queue and the session applies before its ticks);
  - the input, read once per update into an `InputFrame` (keys become `InputAction`s through `KeyBindings`) that feeds both the top screen and Gum's cursor (`Ui/ClientCursor`);
  - `FrameTimings`, each frame's phases (F3 in Debug builds);
  - `WorldView`, how the world is drawn (projection, camera rig and controls, the scene, the map's text, picking): its state moves on in `Update`, and a draw only presents it;
  - the renderer (a silhouette item is drawn through the stock `BasicEffect` with its fog at 100%: its sprite's shape in one flat colour, with no shader of our own), and `Ui/`: the dashboard in Gum, views over the dashboard models that decide nothing. Item icons come from the `IconAtlas` (each icon at 1x and 2x, drawn 1:1, so no texture filter touches them); windows snap to whole pixels so icons land on them. Colours are `UiStyle`'s tokens only. Controls are drawn from the UI kit: `UiParts.Button` and `Slider` are stone (`StoneSkin`, which runs after Gum's own state code), `PrimaryButton` is a window's one amber action, `StoneSkin.SetActive` lights a button while its window is open; `UiParts.Plaque`, `Glyph` and `AddKitFill` place the kit's pieces. The header (`HeaderBar`) is five clusters (below 1440 wide the identity keeps its lantern and drops its words, so the window buttons fit beside the clock: a test checks every offered size), with Save, Load (which asks first), key help and the window's size or full screen (`WindowSizes`; borderless at the screen's own size; the desktop exe keeps the choice in `ClientSettings`' file) in its menu. Gum's canvas follows the window at 1:1 (`EnableExpandToWindow`): a larger window is more room, never larger UI. Motion (`Motion`: windows arrive, the gold rolls) runs only when the host sets `ClientOptions.UiMotion` (the desktop exe), so tests and renders never catch a tween.
- **Rules:** views read the simulation and never write it (a test enforces it), and only `XnaPlatform` may use an API that KNI and MonoGame don't share (`-p:XnaFramework=MonoGame` compiles the client against MonoGame to check).

Namespaces kept the names of the Unity era (`Simpiens.Cognition`, `Simpiens.Simulation`, `Simpiens.Core.Session`, `Simpiens.Entities` for the visual-state enum, …). The directory notes below use the folder names inside `Simpiens.Core` and `Simpiens.Testing.Core`. Every project targets `net10.0` with its default C#, C# 14: .NET 10 is the newest .NET KNI supports (its Android platform targets `net10.0-android`), and the client references the core, so the solution moves to a newer .NET together, when KNI does.

### Directories (`Simpiens.Core`)

- **`Cognition/`**
  Houses the asynchronous cognitive reasoning engine for agents.
  - `Contracts/`: Interfaces and data structures for Agent Intents (`AgentIntent`, `WanderIntent`, `TradeIntent`, `ClaimBountyIntent`, …), Intent Execution (`IIntentHandler`, `IIntentExecutionContext`, `IIntentHandlerRegistry`), and Context (`AgentContext`, `AgentNeeds`, `IntentResult`).
  - `Evaluators/`: Tier-1 reflexive/utility evaluators (`SurvivalUtilityEvaluator` — hunger reflex only, returns `null` otherwise; `MentalBreakEvaluator`, with the danger reflexes; `AltruismEvaluator`, `FrustrationEvaluator`) and the Tier-3 ambient fallback `AmbientWanderEvaluator` (passed to `AgentCognitionService`).
  - `Planning/`: GOAP domain — zero-allocation bitmask `WorldState`/`StateFact`, `GoapPlanner` (A*; the session plans through a `GoapPlannerPool`, one planner per evaluation in flight), `ActionGraph`, `Actions/`, `Goals/`, `WorldStateBuilder`, and modular intent handlers under `Handlers/` (`IntentHandlerRegistry`, `WanderIntentHandler`, `PanicIntentHandler`, `HarvestResourceIntentHandler`, `ShareResourceIntentHandler`, `IdleIntentHandler`, `TradeIntentHandler`, `ClaimBountyIntentHandler`, `DeliverBountyIntentHandler` (Gather + Scout, escrow-only payout; a scout's report publishes its survey), `ScoutIntentHandler`, `SleepAtBunkIntentHandler`, `RetreatIntentHandler`, `TreatWoundsIntentHandler`, `PickUpIntentHandler`, the fight's and the hunt's (`FightIntentHandler`, `HuntIntentHandler`, sharing `Melee`), `CarryIntentHandler`), plus shared `ResourceTargeting` / `BountyQueries` / `BunkTargeting` / `LootTargeting` helpers (a pile a crawler would pick over, from the snapshot's `PileView`s, by `Simulation/Loot/LootRules`: Epic 10 Part 3, K4.3), the utility `Considerations` (personality-weighted priorities, reservation/minimum prices, bounty score) and `Domain/OutpostGoapDomain` (the production action graph and goal list).
  - `Memory/`, `Social/`: Fog-of-war spatial memory, double-buffered snapshots (`IAgentMemory`, `AgentMemorySnapshot`), event ledger, gossip, 4-axis affinity (`AgentMemory`, `AffinityRecord`).
  - `Groups/`: Generic group primitive, directives, compliance, succession (`SuccessionEngine`, `ComplianceUtility`). Groups are many-to-many, one per kind (Epic 10 G4): the registry owns each crawler's `MembershipSet`, ordered by precedence (party, then guild or workshop, then settlement: the order directives are obeyed in; `MembershipSet.Precedence`, since kinds are saved and a new kind takes the next value), and a crawler keeps a copy for cognition. A group led by the player is the patron's: no place on the roster, never succeeded.
  - `Inventory/`: `AgentInventory` (fixed-capacity item slots + gold, double-buffered `AgentInventorySnapshot`), and the worn gear beside the pack (`Gear`, Epic 10 G3: hazard gear, whose durability counts the ticks of hazard exposure it has left).
  - `Pathfinding/`: Zero-allocation 2D Grid A* navigation engine with binary min-heap, epoch tracking, diagonal corner-cutting prevention, and raycast smoothing (`AsyncPathfinder`, `GridAStarSearcher`, `PathRequest`, `PathResponse`).
  - **Key Files**: `AgentState.cs` (pure C# state, needs & drives, health: Epic 10 G2; what it lost in its last tick, `HurtThisTick`, unsaved, for the view's red coat), `Vitality.cs` (health's rules: hazard damage, downed at 0, healing in bunks and from herbs, who ventures into danger; worn gear cuts the damage and wears out in `AgentRuntime`, Epic 10 G3; where a lone crawler goes, a charted lair judged as a hazard, and flight from a creature's blows: `FleeIntent`, pre-empting after exhaustion, Epic 10 Part 3, K2.3), `AgentCognitionService.cs` (cognition: main-thread preparation, off-thread evaluation returning a `CognitionResult`), `CognitionScheduler.cs` (decisions dispatched on tick T land at T + 1 in agent-id order, on the main thread), `AgentConsequenceService.cs` (intent consequences & rewards), `ToolExecutionRegistry.cs` (MCP tool execution bridge), `AgentRuntime.cs` (a crawler's tick).
  - A crawler is plain data (Epic CORE C5): its `AgentState`, its `WorldEntity` in the world and its `AgentRuntime` (the per-tick drives, pre-emption, decision dispatch and perception while busy). Views only read it.

- **`Session/`**
  Sessions as code.
  - An `ISessionDefinition` is a map plus `Populate(SessionBuilder)` (agents, relationships, groups, stalls, seeded bounties). Its `Economy` (`EconomyTuning`) is applied on every respawn, before `Populate`. `OutpostSession` is the live game.
  - `SessionHost` builds any session (loads the map through `IWorldMap`, spawns its keyed nodes, bunks and hearths, calls `Populate`, records the ledger baseline, registers seeded bounties), ticks every crawler after the simulation's tick and then raises `TickCompleted` (where views capture what they draw), and captures and restores it.
  - Every respawn rewinds the clock to tick 0: express seeded expiries relative to the session start. A respawn also starts a new session epoch: an intent stamped with an older `AgentIntent.SessionEpoch` is dropped, because entity ids repeat per seed. The default (outpost) session is picked by id.
  - **A death** (Epic 10 Part 3, K5) leaves the world the drifter's way: `SimulationManager.Die` shares `DepartOutpost`'s release, the body is a pile (`LootPile.BodyOf`), the departed list keeps the cause (`DeathCause`), and a newcomer settles two game days after a resident's death (`SessionHost.NewcomersDue`). Downed in danger (`SimulationManager.InDanger`: a hazard, or a lair with a living creature), a crawler collapses and bleeds out (`AgentState.BleedsOutAt`) unless a friend who hears it comes (`Cognition/Planning/Rescue`, `CarryIntent`, `GiveMedicineIntent`). The dashboard reads the departed through `IDepartedRoll`.
  - The host also admits **drifters** (Epic 10 G1, `SessionHost.Drifters.cs`): strangers on a seeded schedule (`EconomyTuning.Drifters`, `DrifterTuning`) who come in at the map's spawn points with a purse (the `DrifterPurses` faucet), live as crawlers (`AgentState.DepartsAtTick`, 0 for a resident), and leave by deciding to (`LeaveOutpostGoal`, `SimulationManager.DepartOutpost`: their gold leaves as `DrifterDepartures`); the host removes them at the end of the tick and raises `CrawlerDeparted`. Checks that judge "every crawler" judge the residents.
  - `SessionHost.Capture` / `TryRestore` capture and load whole sessions (see `Simulation/Persistence/`); `SaveGameService` is the player's quick-save slot.
  - `Layouts/`: map definitions as code. `Floor1Layout` is the live outpost floor (sectors, hazards, doors, POIs, nodes, spawn points, bunks, lairs), with the Old Stair at its west edge behind the Deep Seam (Epic 10 Part 3, K10.1b: the spinners' web, the Gatekeeper's lair and the sealed descent gate).

- **`Intervention/`**
  Player intervention system acting as an influence queue.
  - **Key Files**: `PlayerInterventionService.cs`, `InfluenceEvent.cs` (zero-allocation typed command union: `PostBountyCommand` (a hunt names a charted lair, Epic 10 Part 3, K6), `CancelBountyCommand`, `UpdateStallPricesCommand`, the guild's `RecruitCommand`, `DismissCommand`, `SetWageCommand`, its parties' `FormPartyCommand`, and its expeditions' `DispatchExpeditionCommand` (a gathering, or a clear of a charted lair: Epic 10 Part 3, K7), `RecallExpeditionCommand` (both naming a party), `GiveGearCommand` (against a hazard, or for a fight); a technique's `SponsorTechniqueCommand` (Epic 11, S4.6: decided by `Simulation/Guild/SponsorRules`); the playtest tools, the dossier's PLAYTEST row under the sway (`ClientOptions.PlaytestTools`: on in development builds, or with the desktop game's `--playtest`), not the player's sway: `CalmCrawlerCommand` (its frustration calmed) and `PlaytestCommand` (a crawler's warmth and trust toward the player at their most, or downed where it stands as by a beetle); and the workshops' `BuildStationCommand`, `HireArtisanCommand`, with `DismissCommand` and `SetWageCommand` reaching an artisan or a member by who it is).

- **`Simulation/`**
  The simulation environment, registries, and spatial representations.
  - `Primitives/`: engine-free value types in the root namespace `Simpiens`: `EntityId`, the id of every simulation entity; and the maths: `Vec2`, `GridPoint`, `RectF`, `Rgba`, `SimMath`. They copy Unity's `Vector2`, `Vector2Int`, `Rect` and `Mathf` formulas exactly (including `Vec2 ==` being approximate and rounding half to even), and float steps are cast where Unity's Mono rounded differently from CoreCLR: the golden baselines depend on both. Keep them.
  - `Spatial/`: Handles memory-managed grids (`SpatialHashGrid`, `SharedWorldSnapshot`, `EntitySnapshot`, `StaticNavGrid` with a static base layer for map walls).
  - `World/`: static geography as plain data. `MapDefinition` (+ `MapDefinitionBuilder`) is the single source of truth for walls, sectors, resource nodes, POIs, stations (where a forge or a still may be built, Epic 10 Part 2) and labels; `WallRasterizer` writes walls into the nav grid; `IWorldMap.Load` sizes the partition from the map and loads `ISectorRegistry` (authoritative sectors + exploration). The snapshot carries the map and the charted-sector mask: **fog of war**, nothing inside an uncharted sector is perceived until a scout's report charts it. A sector may be a **facility** (Epic 10 Part 2, D7: the workshop row, the guildhall): nobody explores it and no bounty targets it. A sector may be a **hazard** (`SectorDefinition.Hazard`, Epic 10 G2): it takes health from whoever stands in it. A crawler alone braves one only up to `Vitality.LoneHazardLimit` (Epic 10 G5), judged through gear made against the hazard's kind (`Vitality.LoneDanger`, Epic 10 Part 2): the Drowned Gallery is an expedition's unless the player sells respirators. Never hard-code coordinates or sector ids elsewhere: read them from the layout.
  - `Items/`: the item system (item refactor I1–I8, 2026-10-26).
    - **What an item is:** `ItemCatalog`, static and immutable, holds one `ItemDefinition` per `ItemType`: its groups as `[Flags] ItemCategory`, its `ItemRarity` (a score that is its default loot weight), names, stack size, sprite key, consume effects (`ConsumeEffect` on a `StatKind`, applied by `Cognition/ItemUse`), and equipment (`EquipmentBlock`: slot, `StatModifier`s, durability and `WearRule`).
    - **Equipment and stats:** a crawler's stats with equipment come from `CrawlerStats` (a pickaxe's mining speed: `CrawlerStats.MiningSpeed`, read by the harvest and by the choice of salvage; protection by hazard kind: `CrawlerStats.HazardResistance(inventory, kind)`, hazard gear's against any hazard plus a respirator's or a hard hat's against its kind). Equipment slots are a table (`EquipmentSlots`, Epic 10 Part 3, K1: each slot's id, kind, body region, draw layer and place on the paper doll; head, body, weapon and tool): an item's `EquipmentBlock` names a `SlotKind`, and `AgentInventory` wears a piece per slot beside the pack, with a record for each of 16 slot ids. What a piece takes the place of is `WornGear`'s rule, which the stall's sales, the guild's gifts and the scripted players share: only the piece in its own slot, so a hard hat and hazard gear are worn together. Every stat stacks over every worn piece (base, plus adds, times multiplies) and is held within its bounds (`StatRules`: protection at most 0.9); every piece wears by its own `WearRule` (exposure, the harvest, a blow landed, a blow taken), whichever slot it is in (`AgentInventory.WearBy`). **Outfitting** is by stats, slot by slot (`Outfitting`, K1.4): a piece is worth what it changes in the wearer's stats for an `OutfitNeed` (the hazards faced, mining), with it worn in its slot (`WornSwap`: nothing moves); residents' purchases (`ResourceTargeting.TryChooseProtection`, `TryChooseWorkGear`), the guild's gifts (`GuildRules.CheckGiveGear`) and the party builder and scripted player's outfit (`GuildRules.PlanOutfit`: the unprotected first, then every slot) all choose by it, never by an item's name or group. A fight's need is in reach (`OutfitNeed.ForFighting`: only a weapon without a range counts) or from range (`ForShooting`: only one with a range); a clear's arms are `GuildRules.PlanArms`, the back of the party's count of shooters armed to shoot (Epic 10 Part 3, K8.2c).
    - **Loot:** loot tables (`Loot.cs`: `LootTable`, `LootTables`, `LootRoller`) are deterministic on `SimRandom`; each creature kind's (`Creatures/CreatureLoot`, Epic 10 Part 3, K4.2) is rolled at its kill into a pile where it fell (`Simulation/Loot/`: `PileService`, `LootPile`). A creature's materials are resources of their own origin (`ItemCategory.Creature`): no node yields them.
    - **Code asks for groups, not items:** `ItemCatalog.Is`, `InCategory`, `InventoryGroups`. `ItemNamesTests` fails on production code that names an item outside content.
    - **How a session trades an item is its economy:** `EconomyTuning`'s `ItemTerms` (base value; the stall's role, opening price and stock) and `SupplyTuning`'s `CaravanTerms` (exports or supplies). A stall's goods are a table (`TradeStallData`'s `StallGoods`), and a price command carries (item, price) pairs.
    - **The client's words for items** are `Simpiens.Presentation/ItemTexts` (an item without an entry gets words from its catalog name); **its pictures** come from `ItemArt` by art key (graphics V3), its icons in the dashboard from `ItemIcons` (the models name an icon key; the client's `IconAtlas` draws it 1:1).
    - **A save** holding an item the catalog doesn't define is refused (`SaveItems`).
  - `People/`: who crawlers are (Epic UI-Beta).
    - `CrawlerLook` is 16 bytes of option ids, 0 = unset, drawn at spawn on its own salt and saved.
    - `LookCatalog` holds the options and the lineages' odds; `GivenNames` holds the name lists and the draw; `DepartedCrawler` is who left.
    - No decision reads a look (`CognitionReadsNoLookTests`) until lineage traits are designed.
  - `Guild/`: the player's guild (Epic 10 G4). `GuildService` owns each member's contract (wage, unpaid visits) and the rejoin cooldowns, reached through `IGuildHost`; `GuildRules` decides the Recruit, Dismiss and SetWage commands and explains a refusal; wages are paid at the caravan's visit (`ISupplyHost.PayGuildWages`). Its numbers are `GuildTuning`. **Parties and expeditions** (Epic 10 G5; Part 2's W5, `GuildService.Expeditions`): the guild holds two party slots (`GuildParty`), each formed by the player and outliving its trips: a `TemporaryParty` group inside the guild, with an `Expedition` directive issued at dispatch. Each party has its own expedition, so both can be out, never in the same sector; the guild moves its stages (muster, march, work, return, deliver) on the main thread from what members' states show, provisions and outfits it from the stall's stock, takes the haul into stock and pays members their shares. Members get a copy of their part each tick (`ExpeditionOrder`), as their memberships. **A clear** (Epic 10 Part 3, K7): a party sent against a charted lair, its march ending at the lair's reach in a fight stage; the guild picks the party's focus and, for a member collapsed in danger, the mate it sends to carry it out, both in the members' orders (`ExpeditionOrder.Focus`, `Carry`); under a clear's orders the party's retreat rule replaces each member's own line (`Vitality.FightLine`); the haul is what was picked up of the lair's material. **The barracks** (`GuildService.Barracks`): the guildhall's bunks (`PoiKind.GuildBunk`), one kept for each member (its contract's `Bunk`, copied to cognition as `KeptBunk`), for a lodging fee taken from its wage.
  - `Workshops/`: the stations (Epic 10 Part 2). `WorkshopService` (`SimulationManager.Workshops`) follows the loaded map's stations and owns each one's `StationState` (built, its order, the current make), saved in its own block; `Crafting` is the rule for one tick of an artisan's work: a make takes its recipe's inputs (`RecipeBook`) from the stall's stock as it begins and puts its output into the stall's stock, or holds it until there is room. `WorkshopRules` decides the Build, HireArtisan, OrderStation and SetShifts commands and explains a refusal; building pays the `Construction` sink; an artisan's contract, crew (a patron-led `Workshop` group at its station) and wages mirror a guild member's. An artisan works in its shifts (`ShiftBlocks`, six blocks of four hours): the crew's `WorkStation` directive is issued per block and followed through Epic 8's compliance, and a run of 10 hours on shift breaks it. Its makes are practice in the station's skill (Epic 10 Part 3, K9): its band is the grade of what it makes (`Grades`: Standard for a Novice, Fine for an Apprentice, Masterwork from Journeyman; `WorkshopService.GradeOf`), and its level quickens them. The stall's shelf keeps each good's graded and worn pieces (`StallGood`), and a repair order mends worn pieces for half their inputs. Its numbers are `WorkshopTuning`.
  - `Movement/`: `Locomotion`, how a pawn moves (path and panic steps, the motor watchdog, crowding and wall checks); `SimulationManager` owns one and forwards to it.
  - `Creatures/`: creature kinds as data (Epic 10 Part 3, K0.4). `CreatureCatalog`, static and immutable as the item catalogue is, holds one `CreatureDefinition` per `CreatureKind`: health, attack, defense, reach, swing interval and range (read through `Combatant.ForCreature`), speed, body, sight, leash, the flight line, pack size, and healing and return in game hours. A new kind is a `CreatureKind` value (never reused: creatures will be saved by kind, K2.4) and a row; its picture is a pattern in `CreatureSprites` and a row in `TopDownArtTable.Creatures` (both view modes), by its art key (`ArtCompletenessTests` fails a kind without). **Lairs and the creatures' rule** (K2): the map's lairs (`LairDefinition`) are filled on the seed; `CreatureService` (`SimulationManager.Creatures`) runs the creatures' phase after the crawlers' runtime, in id order (rest, roam, chase within the leash, strike, flee, go home), every swing decided before any harm lands. A lair's reach is its kind's leash around its heart, and its danger a pack's expected harm a second (`LairReach`). A lair is charted, known to everyone, when a crawler sees one of its creatures in a charted sector or is struck by one (`ChartedMask`, in the snapshot), and a lone crawler keeps out of a charted lair's reach as out of a hazard (`Vitality.WouldEnterAt`). The creatures are saved in the world's block (`CreatureSave`). **Fighting back** (K3.3): struck, the bold and armed fight (`FightIntent`, `Vitality.FightsBack`) and the rest flee; a crawler's swing is asked of the creatures' pass (`SwingAtCreature`), so every blow of a tick is decided before any harm lands; a bold, armed crawler braves a known lair it expects to clear (`Vitality.HarmToClear`, `BravesLair`); the bold buy the best weapon they can afford (`ArmForFightGoal`, `TryChooseWeapon`, by `OutfitNeed.ForFighting`). Hurt below the treat line in a fight, a crawler drinks a draught it carries in place of a swing (`Melee.TryQuaff`, K10.6, D69), and a crawler's travel and harvest paths are judged along the way against charted lairs, not only where they end (`Vitality.WouldWalk`, D70). **Bosses** (K10): rows with traits (`IsBoss`, `Slam`, `FuryBelow`, `CallsBroodBelow`, `WholeAfterHours`); the rule reads the traits, never a kind's name. A lair may name a boss in its first place (`LairDefinition.Boss`: the Matriarch heads the warren), judged with her only while she lives (the snapshot's `BossLairMask`); a lair that `GuardsGate` is charted with its sector, and emptied for good it breaches the gate (`WorldFlags.GateBreached`, saved in the world's block). A slam's rolls are `CombatRules.Slam` (one draw a foe in id order); a keeper's last visitor's tick (`CreatureState.LastVisitAt`) is saved, so it stands whole again after its hour across a load. **Named creatures** (Epic 11, S7): a creature whose blow downs a crawler takes a name from it ("Ama's Bane": `CreatureTitleTable`, a row per rank), and each further crawler it downs raises its rank (`CreatureService.Credit`, called where the blow lands and where the fallen dies); a boss never does. A rank grows its health and attack (the table's columns, read by `Combatant.ForCreature` and `CreatureState.MaxHealth`), makes its lair more dangerous to judge (the snapshot's `LeaderRank`, read by `LairReach.Danger` and `Vitality.HarmToClear`), and its pack mates in the lair's reach take its quarry while it chases. Its record keeps whom it was named for, its rank and its foe; the session host registers its name with the identity service, as a crawler's (`SessionHost.NameCreatures`).
  - `Skills/`: skills as a table (Epic 10 Part 3, K9; a namespace of its own, since cognition reads skills and may read nothing of `People/`). Their rules, numbers and measurements are in [`docs/skills-model.md`](docs/skills-model.md), which Epic 11 keeps up to date.
    - **What a skill is:** `SkillTable`, static and immutable as the slots' table is, holds one `SkillDefinition` per `Skill`: its words, the work that practises it (`SkillUse`: a melee or ranged fight, a harvest of an item group, a station's makes, tending the fallen, leading a party) and the personality that leans its draw as a talent. Leadership (Epic 11, S5.1) is practised by a party's captain (`GuildService.CaptainOf`) at a trip's delivery and steadies the party's retreat line (`GuildService.RetreatLine`). Code names the work it does (`SkillWork`: a fight in reach or from range, a harvest of an item, a station's makes, tending the fallen), never a skill (`SkillNamesTests`); the table finds the work's skill (`SkillTable.SkillFor`).
    - **Levels and practice:** `SkillRules` holds the levels (0–10, a level costing 100 times its number), the bands (Novice 0–2, Apprentice 3–5, Journeyman 6–8, Master 9–10), what each act is worth (a blow landed, a shot, a unit harvested, a make, a rescue) and what a level does. A crawler's `SkillSet` (16 experiences, saved in its record) is drawn at spawn on its own salt: each skill a Novice's, one or two talents an Apprentice's. Practice enters only through `ISkillPractice` (`SimulationManager.Practise`, which the intent context, the creatures' world and the workshops' host carry), by the work done and its source (`PracticeSource`: the work itself, today, with the highest level a source can teach; K9.8a); a band reached is told in the chronicle.
    - **The seam** (K9.8a): how good a crawler is at its work is `CrawlerSkills.Level(skills, worn, work)`, its effective level, a pure function of what cognition's copy also holds, so the main thread and cognition agree. Every effect and choice reads it; the learned level (`SkillSet.Level`) is what practice raises, the save holds and the dossier lists (`SkillReadsTests`). Today the two are equal: gear, talents or a family that change how good a crawler is at its work enter in `CrawlerSkills`, and a family or a narrower skill in `SkillFor` and `Practise`, with no change to the work's callers.
    - **What a level does:** combat's hit chance and damage (read through `Combatant`); the harvest's pace, centred on a Novice 2 and multiplied with a pickaxe's (`HarvestResourceIntentHandler.SkillSpeed`, which the salvage choice weighs too); a make's grade and speed; a rescuer's carrying pace and a medicine's healing. At level 0 a crawler fights, makes and tends by the numbers before skills.
  - `Combat/`: the combat rule (Epic 10 Part 3). `CombatRules` reads a fighter only through `Combatant` (a crawler's from its state and `CrawlerStats`, a creature's from its kind and record), rolls each swing on its own seeded stream (`SimRandomSalt.Combat`, keyed by the attacker and the tick), cuts a hit by `Defense` (D / (D + 10)) and wears a weapon per landed hit. Pure functions: whoever owns a fighter keeps when it may swing next, and a pass decides every swing of a tick before any harm lands, so no outcome depends on entity ids. Combat runs in physical time (ticks), not the day's minutes. **Ranged** (K8): a fighter with a range (`StatKind.Range`: the sling, the crossbow, a spinner) strikes within it, its chance falling past half its range (`ShotChance`), its weapon wearing per shot (`WearRule.PerShot`); a shot past reach needs a clear line (the snapshot's ray, checked in the creatures' pass); a shooter keeps its distance (`CombatRules.KeepAway`: a crawler's in the fight's handlers, `Ranged`; a creature's in its rule). Each tick's shots are `CreatureService.ShotsThisTick` (`ShotRecord`, unsaved), which the frames carry for the scene to draw.
  - **The day** (Epic 10 Part 2, milestone T; [`docs/time-model.md`](docs/time-model.md)): `DayClock` is the 24-hour day's arithmetic, RimWorld-paced: 10,080 ticks a day (1,008 s at 1×), a game minute 7 ticks, an hour 420, tick 0 at 06:00 on day 1. A crawler's pace is the session's `DayTuning` (inside `EconomyTuning`): needs, sleep and its wake lines, rest, healing, trust's fade, and how long each action takes (`ActionTuning`).
    - **Two kinds of time.** Physical time (walking, poses, perception, cognition's latency, retries, ambient idling) stays in ticks and seconds. The day's time (actions, needs, sleep, hazards, healing, wear, the caravan, regrowth, drifters, cooldowns, bounty lives, the expedition's stages, recipes, shifts) is written in game minutes and hours through `DayClock` and the pace, never as tick literals.
    - **Checks run in game time too:** "the outpost's day" is `OutpostWatch.DayTicks`; validators that run an action use `HeadlessIntents.RunToCompletion`, since a trade or a harvest takes game minutes.
  - `Economy/`: Unmanaged economy structs (`ItemInstance`, `TradeStallData`, `BountyContract`, and beside a hunt's contract its `HuntRecord`: the lair and up to three hunters with the damage each dealt, paid from escrow by damage when the lair is empty, Epic 10 Part 3, K6), the stalls and the bounty board (`Market`: escrow, expiry, the economy save block; `SimulationManager` owns one and forwards to it), authoritative gold tracking (`IEconomyLedger`, `EconomyLedger`), the session's economy numbers (`EconomyTuning`, with `SupplyTuning`, `DrifterTuning` and `GearTuning` nested), the trade fairness rule (`TradeFairness`), and the periodic economy (`ISupplyService`, `SupplyService`). The periodic economy covers dormant-node regrowth and the caravan visit: salvage quota faucet, then upkeep sink, then ration restock. `SimulationManager` ticks it via `ISupplyHost`. The model is in [`docs/economy-model.md`](docs/economy-model.md). Economy numbers live in `EconomyTuning` only: read them from the session's tuning (`IIntentExecutionContext.Economy`, or `Considerations.Economy` in cognition), never as literals.
  - `Persistence/`: session save and load.
    - `SessionSave` is a whole session at one tick: a header, then one block per owner (crawlers, economy, world, groups, chronicle). Each owner writes and reads its own block (`CaptureTo` / `RestoreFrom`, `AgentState.CaptureRecord` / `RestoreRecord`); `SessionHost.Capture` / `TryRestore` assemble them. Keyed records are sorted by id, so a capture doesn't depend on dictionary order.
    - **New authoritative state goes into its owner's block,** with a check in the round-trip validators (`SessionCaptureValidator`, `SessionRestoreValidator`). A round trip only proves what a capture contains: state left out of the save is lost on load without any test failing.
    - Not saved: work in progress (intents, plans, and the bunk claims and contention tied to them: crawlers decide afresh after a load), derived state (snapshots, grids, the agent and world registries), and what the session definition provides (the map and the economy tuning).
    - A load rebuilds the map exactly as a respawn does (same ids for the map's entities), then restores in place of `Populate`. It refuses, before changing anything, a save with another format version, session, seed or map (`SaveFingerprint`).
    - Saves are captured and loaded on the main thread between ticks, never from cognition. Nothing a save reads is written off-thread.
    - `SaveFile` is the file format: a plain header (magic, format version, length, checksum), then the save, so a wrong, cut-off or damaged file is refused with a reason. `SaveGameService` (`Session/`) is the quick-save slot, `Saves/quicksave.simsave` under the per-user application data folder; tests give it their own folder.
    - `SaveSerializer` is the only code that calls MemoryPack.
    - Unmanaged records are saved as raw memory copies, so changing a saved struct's layout changes the save format. When that happens, bump `SessionSaveHeader.CurrentFormatVersion` (`EconomySerializationValidator` pins the economy records' sizes).
  - **Key Files**: `SimulationManager.cs` and its partial files by domain (`SimulationManager.Market.cs`, `.World.cs`, `.Lodging.cs`, `.Social.cs`, `.Movement.cs`, `.Persistence.cs`) (the main-thread tick: an orchestrator implementing `IIntentExecutionContext`, delegating intent execution to `IIntentHandlerRegistry`, draining typed `InfluenceEvent`s, trade/bounty/contention rules, hearth dynamics), `WorldEntity.cs` (an entity as plain data: id, kind, position, radius, speed, resource kind, its `AgentState` for crawlers), `WorldRegistry.cs` (entities in registration order, which is the snapshot's order; `EntityRegistered`/`EntityUnregistered` let views show and hide), `GroupRegistry.cs`, `SimulationClock.cs`, `ISimulationManager.cs`.

### Adding or removing an item
- **Add:**
  1. An `ItemType` value. Never reuse one: a removed item's id stays reserved.
  2. A definition in `ItemCatalog`: groups, rarity, and its consume or equipment block if it has one.
  3. Its terms in the session's economy, if the stall or the caravan trades it (`EconomyTuning`'s item terms, `SupplyTuning`'s caravan terms).
  - If a station makes it: its recipe in `RecipeBook` (its station, inputs and work time; one recipe per item, Epic 10 Part 2).
  4. Its look: one line in `ItemArt`, by its art key (`ItemDefinition.ArtKey`, the name in kebab case): a shape from `ItemShapes` (draw a new 12x12 one only if none fits), its two colours, how it is worn. Its icon, map sprite and carried marker follow; a node row in the view modes' tables if a map node yields it. Look at the art review sheet, then re-record the art pins.
  5. The map nodes that yield it.
  6. Optionally, bespoke words in `ItemTexts`.
  - Nothing else: the stall, the caravan, the crawlers and the merchant terminal follow (`ItemSystemProofTests` adds one this way).
  - If the stall's goods would pass `TradeStallData.GoodsCapacity` (8), raise it: that changes the save format.
- **Take out of the economy:** drop its stall and caravan terms (glowstone was taken out this way in `ItemSystemProofTests`).
- **Remove from the game:** delete its definition and mark its `ItemType` value retired; saves holding it are refused on load.
- **Quirks kept on purpose.** Each is a behaviour change to make deliberately, with its golden re-record in its own commit; `ItemNamesTests` lists where they name items.
  1. A ration takes 40 off hunger from the pack, but 50 when eaten at a Garden plot (a node rule).
  2. Setting `AgentState.HasFoodInInventory` true packs a ration.
  3. A node yielding `ItemType.None` counts as food.
  4. `ItemInstance.ItemId` is stamped 1 everywhere.

### Adding an equipment slot
- **A slot is a row** (Epic 10 Part 3, K1; the producer's "appendable equipment system"):
  1. An `EquipmentSlot` value under `EquipmentSlots.Capacity` (16). Never reuse one: the id is the slot's record in a crawler's save, and protection's 0 stays retired.
  2. Its row in `EquipmentSlots`: code name, the player's word, the `SlotKind` it takes, its `BodyRegion`, its draw layer (worn looks stack up the layers), its cell on the paper doll and its place in lists (`Order`).
  3. A new kind of piece is a `SlotKind` value (never reused); two slots may take one kind (two hands for rings), and a piece goes in the first free one.
  4. The items that fit it: their `EquipmentBlock` names the kind, with their stat modifiers and `WearRule` ("Adding or removing an item").
  5. If its pieces show worn, their look in `ItemArt` says where (`Worn`: on the head, over the face, on the torso). A part of the body no mode draws yet is a new `Worn` value and its drawing in `BodyKit`, `PortraitKit` and `WornSprites`; look at the people sheet and re-record the art pins.
  6. If a stat needs bounds, a row in `StatRules`.
- **Nothing else:** stats and their stacking, wear by rule, outfitting by stats (residents' purchases, the guild's gifts, the party builder), saves (all 16 records, so an older save loads the new slot empty), and the dossier's and the guild cards' gear lines follow (`EquipmentSlotProofTests` in both test projects add feet and boots this way; `TestCatalogs.FeetAndBoots`).
- **The checks:** `EquipmentSlotsValidator` (the table, the save's records); `SlotNamesTests` (production code names a slot only in the table and the catalogue: ask `EquipmentSlots`, `WornGear` and `Outfitting`).
- **A 17th slot** changes the save format: raise `EquipmentSlots.Capacity` and bump `SessionSaveHeader.CurrentFormatVersion` (the size pin in `EconomySerializationValidator` fails until you do).

### Adding a skill
- **A skill is a row** (Epic 10 Part 3, K9; the producer's Q17, "appendable, like the slots"):
  1. A `Skill` value under `SkillTable.Capacity` (16). Never reuse one: the id is the skill's place in a crawler's `SkillSet` and in its save.
  2. Its row in `SkillTable`: code name, the player's word, the work that practises it (`SkillUse`; for a harvest, the item group it harvests, `Harvests`; for making, the station, `Station`), the personality that leans its draw (`Lean`), its practitioner's word for the chronicle and its place in lists (`Order`).
  3. A new kind of work is a `SkillUse` value and its code: a `SkillWork` for it (and its case in `SkillTable.SkillFor` if the work has a qualifier, as a harvest's item or a station), one `Practise` call where the work is done (`ISkillPractice`, with `PracticeSource.Work`) with what it is worth, and what a level does, a factor in `SkillRules` read through `CrawlerSkills.Level` where the work is done.
- **Nothing else:** the draw at spawn, practice, what a level does for its kind of work, the save (all 16 experiences, so an older save loads the new skill at 0), the dossier's and the roster's lines and the chronicle's band reached follow (`SkillProofTests` in both test projects add Mycology, the harvest of a new group, this way; `TestCatalogs.MycologyAndGlowcaps`).
- **The checks:** `SkillsValidator` (the table, the levels and bands, the seam, the draw and its lean); `SkillNamesTests` (production code names a skill only in the table: name the work); `SkillReadsTests` (production code reads learned levels only in the seam, practice's entry and the dossier's list).
- **A new skill changes every seed's draw** (the talents are picked among all skills): re-record golden in its own commit.
- **A 17th skill** changes the save format: raise `SkillTable.Capacity` and bump `SessionSaveHeader.CurrentFormatVersion`.

### Who a crawler has become: the traits record
- **One record** (Epic 11, A3): `CrawlerTraits` (`Simulation/Skills/`), 256 bytes beside the crawler's `SkillSet`, saved whole in its record (format 12) and copied for cognition (`AgentContext.Traits`). It holds passions, techniques, teachers, deeds, a calling, an epithet, earned traits and scars, and room left over.
- **A new field** goes into the record's spare bytes, so its size and the save's format don't change (`EconomySerializationValidator` pins 256). The restore validator checks the round trip, and the world hash mixes the record once anything is written.
- **The numbers and measurements** are in [`docs/skills-model.md`](docs/skills-model.md).

### Passions, challenge and the cost of mastery
- **A passion** (Epic 11, S1): a step of `PassionTable` (none, keen, burning), saved in the traits record. A new step is a `Passion` value and a row: its practice multiplier, the frustration it eases, its weight in the work's choice and its UI kit glyph (`UiKit`, then the art pins). The draw's odds are `PassionTable`'s constants.
- **The mastery budget** (Epic 11, S2): at most two skills past Apprentice and one at Master, held in practice's one entry beside the source's ceiling (`SkillRules.BudgetCeiling`). `OutpostWatch` fails a live scenario's day on any breach.
- **The asking wage** (Epic 11, S2): a member's or artisan's reservation wage raised by its best band (`GuildRules.AskingWage`, `WorkshopRules.AskingWage`; the raise rows are `GuildTuning`'s). Signing, the visit's pay, the chronicle's ask and the dashboard read it; the scripted players pay raises unless told `LeaveRaisesUnpaid`.
- **A challenge** is the level an act demands (`PracticeSource.WorkAt(challenge)`): a column on each creature kind (`CreatureDefinition.Challenge`), harvested item (`ItemDefinition.HarvestChallenge`) and recipe (`Recipe.Challenge`). A new kind, item or recipe sets its own (`SkillsValidator` requires one on every creature, salvage and recipe).

### Teachers (Epic 11, S3)
- **Practice's sources:** the work itself (`PracticeKind.Work`), a mentor (`Mentor`: a better hand at the same work nearby, judged in practice's one entry), a manual (`Manual`: read at the hearth) and the drill yard (`Yard`). A new source is a `PracticeKind` value and one `Practise` call with its ceiling; the passion and the budget apply to every source alike.
- **A teacher's lineage** is the traits record's four teacher slots: the first teacher of each skill, recorded at the first lesson, with the mentor's "taught" deed.
- **Adding a manual** is an item by "Adding or removing an item", with a `ManualBlock` (the work it teaches, its experience, its ceiling) and `ItemCategory.Manual`; its terms (the stall sells it, the caravan supplies it into `ManualWholesale`). Nothing else: who buys it (`ResourceTargeting.TryChooseManual`: passions only, below the ceiling) and the reading follow. A treatise is a primer's block with another ceiling.
- **The drill yard** is a station kind (`StationKind.DrillYard`) with no recipes and no artisan, built for `WorkshopTuning.DrillYardCost`; the snapshot carries which stations stand (`IsStationBuilt`), and cognition finds it through `Drill`.

### Adding a technique
- **A technique is a row** (Epic 11, S4, A2, D13):
  1. A `Technique` value (a byte). Never reuse one: the id is what a crawler's traits record holds in its band's slot, and its save.
  2. Its row in `TechniqueTable`: the player's words, its skill and band (Apprentice, Journeyman or Master), the personality that leans its pick, its vow (the crawler's line when it picks it) and two or three effects, each a `TechniqueStat` and a value, at least one an upside and one a cost.
  3. Two at each band: a band's other row is what a sponsor offers in its place (`TechniqueTable.OtherThan`).
  - A new kind of effect is a `TechniqueStat` value with its bounds, the work it applies to and its words in `TechniqueRules` (Latin-1 only: the client's fonts hold no more), and one read through `CrawlerTechniques.Get` where the work is done.
- **Nothing else:** the pick (by nature, at a band reached and at spawn: `TechniqueRules.FillReached`, on `SimRandomSalt.Techniques`), the effects (the door, `CrawlerTechniques.Get`: summed over the techniques held in the work's skill, within bounds; the fight's fighter, the harvest's, make's and carry's pace, a make's grade, healing, practice, a mentor's lessons), sponsorship (`SponsorTechniqueCommand`, decided by `SponsorRules`), the save, the dossier's line, the technique tree (`TechniqueTreeModel`, the client's `TechniqueTreeWindow`: its node for the row, in its skill's tree at its band) and the choice scene (`SkillScenes`) follow. The tree holds two at each band: `TechniqueTreeTests` finds every row in a node, and checks that its words fit it.
- **The checks:** `TechniqueValidator` (the table, the bounds, the traits record's slots); `TechniquePickValidator` (the pick's odds by nature); `TechniqueEffectsValidator` (a test per kind of effect); `SponsorValidator`; `TechniqueNamesTests` (production code names a technique only in the table); `TechniqueReadsTests` (effects read techniques only through the door).

### Adding a calling
- **A calling is a row** (Epic 11, S5, A2, D20):
  1. A `Calling` value (a byte). Never reuse one: the id is what a crawler's traits record holds and its save.
  2. Its row in `CallingTable`: the player's word, its skill, the Apprentice technique of that skill that earns it (none for a skill without techniques), its party role (`PartyRole`), its Master title and its capstone (a `TechniqueEffect`, or none).
  3. A new role is a `PartyRole` value, its word and glyph (`CallingTexts`), its badge (`BadgeSprites`, a row in both view modes' tables, then the art pins), and where the guild's orders or the creatures' rule read it (`ExpeditionOrder.Role`).
- **Nothing else:** the earning (`CallingRules.Earned`: the best skill and its Apprentice technique, kept while it ties), the chronicle, the capstone through `CrawlerTechniques.Get`, the save, the titles on every card and the badge follow.
- **The checks:** `CallingValidator` (the table, what earns each, roles, titles, capstones, the rule); `PartyRoleValidator` (the roles' rules); `CallingNamesTests` (production code names a calling only in the table: read its role).

### Adding a deed
- **A deed is a row** (Epic 11, A7, D2):
  1. A `Deed` value under `DeedTable.Capacity` (16). Never reuse one: the id is the deed's counter in a crawler's traits record and in its save.
  2. Its row in `DeedTable`: code name and the player's words for its count.
  3. One count where the deed happens, on the main thread (`AgentState.AddDeed`). Never rebuild a deed from the chronicle.
- **The checks:**
  - `DeedsValidator`: the table and the counters;
  - `DeedNamesTests`: a deed is named only in the table and at the places listed there, and every deed is counted somewhere. List the new place.
  - Prove it in the live scenario that brings the deed about (`OutpostWatch.DeedTotal`).
- **A 17th deed** changes the save format.

### Adding art
- **Art for a known kind of thing** (a tile, a prop, a node, a crawler state) is a sprite and a table row:
  1. The sprite, by name, in a generator (`ProceduralSprites`, `IsometricSprites`; an item's come from `ItemArt`, above). A new generator's sprites go into their sheet in `SpriteSheets`. Generators never read a table or a sheet: an initialisation cycle crashes depending on which class loads first (`ArtInitialisationTests`).
  2. A row under its `ArtKeys` key in each view mode's table (`TopDownArtTable`, `IsometricArtTable`): frames, size in units of a scale, pivot, frame rate, pulse. Layout that belongs to the art (where carried goods hang, the stall's display slots) is the catalogue's data too.
- **A new kind of thing** also needs `EntityVisuals` to say which key it is drawn with.
- **The checks:** `ArtCompletenessTests` (everything the frames carry has art, every row is used, every node on a shipped map has its own); `AtlasLayoutTests` (each sheet within its 2048 budget). Then look at the art review sheet, re-record the art pins in their own commit, and re-record the changed renders in the pinned image (see "Checking a change").

### Adding a look
- **An option** (a hair style, a skin tone, a colour, an outfit) is a key at the end of its gene's list in `LookCatalog` and its odds in the lineages that offer it. Never reorder or remove one: retire it by giving it no odds anywhere, so a saved look keeps its meaning.
- **A lineage** is a key at the end of the lineage list and a `LineageDefinition` with its odds per gene.
- **A gene** goes last in `LookGene` and is drawn last, so every seed's earlier genes keep their values. It uses one of `CrawlerLook`'s spare bytes; a 17th gene changes the save format.
- **Its picture:**
  - on the map (`Simpiens.Presentation/Art/People/`): a colour is a row in `LookPalettes`; a hair style, facial hair or build is a case in `BodyKit`, drawn at each frame's head anchor (`HeadAnchors`; a new pose frame is a row there);
  - in the portrait: a case in `PortraitKit` (a hair style's front and back, an outfit's cut, an accessory, a mood's features);
  - then look at the people sheet and re-record the art pins (they hold 151 bodies).
- **The checks:**
  - `LookValidator`: the catalogue, and every option at its odds.
  - The people sheet: look at it.
  - `CrawlerIdentityTests`: every option turns up on the gate seeds.
  - A change of odds changes every seed's looks: re-record golden in its own commit (only hashes move).
- **A name** is a line in `GivenNames`: Latin-1 letters, at most 10 characters, in one list only (`GivenNamesValidator`; the client's `FontTests` checks the glyphs).

## Version Control (commit discipline)
Work is saved through regular, reviewable commits. Uncommitted progress spanning several sessions is a defect.
1. **Commit at the end of every completed session or task.** Also commit after each self-contained step of a multi-phase plan (a phase of `.agents/*_plan.md`, a fix plus its validator, a docs reconciliation). Never end a session with completed work only in the working tree.
2. **Commit a working state.** The solution builds, with no warnings: warnings are errors (`dotnet/Directory.Build.props`), so fix a new one rather than suppress it. State the test status honestly in the commit body (e.g. "dotnet test 372/372; golden 3/3"). Never claim tests passed that were not run.
3. **Keep commits logical and scoped.** Separate dependency/tooling changes, feature code and docs where practical. Use the existing Conventional Commits style: `feat(scope):`, `fix(scope):`, `docs(scope):`, `chore(scope):`, `test(scope):`.
4. **Never commit generated artifacts:** `bin/`, `obj/`, `Logs/`, `container-output/`. `.gitignore` covers these. If a new kind of generated output appears, extend `.gitignore` in the same commit.
5. **Branching:** follow the user's instruction. When none is given, do not commit large multi-session work directly to `main`; use a descriptive branch (`feat/<topic>`) and say so. Target pull requests at `main`, not at another feature branch.
6. **Never** force-push, rewrite published history, or commit secrets/credentials.

## Documentation Protocol (read before changing status or scope)
1. **Roadmap first.** When an epic's status or scope changes, update `docs/ROADMAP.md` *first*, then the epic's `Status` header in `docs/epics/`, then `.agents/curentfocus.txt`.
2. **Use the status vocabulary** defined in the roadmap §0: `QUEUED`, `ACTIVE`, `PAUSED`, `MECHANISM COMPLETE`, `COMPLETED`.
3. **Never restate status in this file or in code comments.** Link to the roadmap.
4. **Acceptance checklists list only delivered work.** Anything dropped or postponed goes under an explicit **Deferred** heading naming the epic that owns it.
5. `.agents/architecture_history.md` is an append-only decision log. Add entries; don't rewrite history.
6. **Plans in `.agents/*_plan.md` are working documents.** Keep only the plans for work in progress. When a plan's work is complete and its docs are reconciled:
   - move its open findings to the epic doc;
   - move data still needed for a decision (soak numbers, measurements) to `docs/`;
   - summarise its decisions and deviations in `architecture_history.md`;
   - then delete the plan, and link nothing to it. Git keeps it (`git log --diff-filter=D -- .agents/`).
7. **Every implementation plan is stamped with a complexity grade (hard rule).**
   - The grades are `Basic`, `Possibly Complex` and `Very Complex`.
   - The plan has one in its header. So do each of its phases and each of its steps, and the remaining milestones of an epic.
   - A plan without stamps is not ready to execute.
   - A Very Complex step or phase is split further, or is marked as supervised by the developer.
   - The rubric and the rules are in [`.agents/rules/complexity-grading.md`](.agents/rules/complexity-grading.md).

## Definition of Done
**"Done" means the feature *emerges in the live simulation*, not only that a validator passes.**

- Headless validators prove a mechanism in isolation, usually by **injecting preconditions** (hand-placed inventory, teleported agents, forced intents, pre-claimed bounties). That is a *mechanism test*, not evidence the game works.
- Before an epic/slice may be marked `COMPLETED`, add at least one **live scenario** in `dotnet/Simpiens.Core.Tests` that runs on the real session setup, injects **none** of the outcome's preconditions, and asserts a concrete outcome within a bounded tick count (e.g. *"within N ticks a posted Gather bounty is claimed, fulfilled and paid out"*). A scripted player that acts only through typed player commands is input, not injection.
- With only validators passing, the status is `MECHANISM COMPLETE`.
- Full criteria: `docs/ROADMAP.md` §1B.

## Multiplayer-Readiness (binding for new simulation work)
Multiplayer is a **required post-MVP feature**. The MVP is single-player, but do not make choices that prevent an authoritative client-server model later. In short (full list: `docs/ROADMAP.md` §1A.1):
- Authoritative state advances on **fixed logical ticks** (`ISimulationClock`); express durations/cooldowns in **ticks**, never frame time. The day's durations are written in game minutes and hours through `DayClock` (which turns them into ticks) and the session's `DayTuning`; motion and what is shown stay in ticks ([`docs/time-model.md`](docs/time-model.md)).
- Actions enter the simulation only as **typed, serializable commands/intents** applied in a **deterministic order** (not thread-pool completion order, not `Dictionary` enumeration order).
- Simulation randomness uses a **seeded** per-simulation RNG (`SimRandom`) — no unseeded `System.Random` in simulation decisions.
- Authoritative state is **plain data**; views read it and never own it.
- **Single writer:** background threads read immutable snapshots/copies; all mutation happens on the simulation thread.

The code has violated some of the rules above and below in places; `docs/ROADMAP.md` §1A.2 records each deviation and how it was resolved. When touching these areas, don't extend the deviation; follow the rules, and flag the conflict rather than silently entrenching it.

## Logging (binding; full rules in `docs/ROADMAP.md` §1A.3)
- Use `SimLog.<Channel>.ZLog<Level>($"...")` and never `Console.WriteLine` in application code. Validators log through `TestLog` and fail by throwing, so any test framework can run them.
- Build nothing outside the interpolation holes: a disabled call must allocate nothing. Gate per-agent decision traces with `SimLog.Traces(channel, level, agentId)` and label entities with `SimLog.Agent(id)`.
- Levels:
  - **Error:** broken invariants.
  - **Warning:** rejected or degraded operations.
  - **Information:** player-meaningful events.
  - **Debug:** per-agent decisions.
  - **Trace:** per-candidate detail.
- Enable verbose output with `SIMPIENS_LOG="Planning=Debug;Console=Warn" SIMPIENS_LOG_FILE=Logs/sim.log SIMPIENS_LOG_FOCUS=Crawler`.

## Architecture Constraints Reminder
- **Composition over Inheritance**: C# interfaces and constructor injection; the core is container-agnostic, and `Microsoft.Extensions.DependencyInjection` composes it in the hosts (`AddSimpiensCore`).
- **Views decide nothing**: the client's views and dashboard models read frames and send typed commands.
- **Zero Allocations in Hot Paths**: Use Structs, object pooling, and standard `for` loops in tick-critical areas.
- **Dual-Loop System**: Use `SimulationManager` for the fixed logical tick, and `AgentCognitionService` for background cognition. Off-thread evaluation writes nothing on `AgentState`: it returns its decision, and the `CognitionScheduler` applies it on the main thread.
- **Defensive Null-Safety for Test Isolation**: Core domain services (`SimulationManager`, `AgentRuntime`, `AgentCognitionService`) must be null-safe regarding their injected interfaces (`ISpatialPartition`, `IWorldRegistry`, `ISimulationClock`). Validators instantiate these services directly in isolation with `null` dependencies. Never dereference an injected dependency without a null guard or fallback (e.g., `_spatialPartition == null` returns unblocked/empty, `_clock == null` defaults to tick 0).



## Refactor and Reevaluate always

Whenever you are tasked with changing or editing an existing system and need to do a refactor always do 2 things:
- 1) Determine the complexity of this refactor
- 2) Determine if this task would be much less complex if the architecture was cleaner

If you determine it is a complex task and that it would be much less complex if the code was structured in a better way then flag it. Do not simply complete the task as written but determine how much benefit you would be giving the repo by improving the architecture first. If the developer is around flag it for them to review or if you have a directive to complete a long sprint of work then use your best judgement and bring it up in your post done report.
